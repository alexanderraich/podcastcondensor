You are writing the spoken narration of one episode of "Lord of Spirits", an
Orthodox Christian podcast. The listener is driving or walking and cannot look
anything up. Your text will be read aloud by a synthetic voice, so it must work
as continuous prose, heard once.

Below is the episode's ARC: a map of the movements the hosts made, what each
one establishes, what merely restates earlier material, and what it connects to
from earlier episodes. You do NOT have the transcript. Write from the arc.

WHAT YOU ARE PRODUCING
A condensed telling of this episode: the substance of what the hosts
established, in a form a listener can follow from the first sentence to the
last. Not a list of topics — a connected exposition that carries the argument.

THREE RULES, IN ORDER OF IMPORTANCE

1. REPETITION WITHIN THIS EPISODE — CUT IT. Where the arc marks a movement as
   restating something already said earlier in this episode, drop it. Say the
   thing once, well, and move on. A movement that is pure restatement earns no
   sentence at all.

2. BACKGROUND FROM EARLIER EPISODES — KEEP IT. Where the arc records a
   "backrefs", the listener may not have heard that earlier episode. Do not
   assume knowledge you have no reason to assume. A sentence of context that
   quietly connects the current point to something established earlier in the
   podcast is valuable and must survive. This is the opposite of rule 1: a
   recap of what was said twenty minutes ago is padding, but a connection to
   something from years ago that the listener half-remembers is orientation.

3. Q&A — JUDGE IT ON MERIT. Where the arc marks a movement as Q&A, keep it only
   if the arc gives a reason ("qa_merit"). Redundant questions where the hosts
   scramble for an answer they have already given are dropped without ceremony.
   An exchange that opened something genuinely new is kept, and told.

HOW LONG
Roughly 2,600 to 3,600 words — about fifteen to twenty minutes spoken. This is
a zone, not a quota, and it is a ceiling as much as a floor.

Do NOT allocate a fixed share of the words to each movement. Follow the
substance: a movement the arc marks "high" may take several paragraphs; a
movement that is setup or a brief illustration may take one sentence; a
movement that only restates gets nothing. If this episode genuinely has less in
it than most, come in under the zone and finish. Do not pad, and do not stretch
a thin episode to reach a number. Say what there is to say.

SHAPE
Open by orienting the listener: what this episode is about, and what question
it is answering. Develop the movements in the order the arc gives them, and
carry the argument forward rather than jumping between topics. Close wherever
the episode actually landed.

STYLE
- Plain flowing prose. No headings, no bullet points, no numbered lists.
- No timestamps, no episode numbers, no meta-commentary about the podcast or
  about your own narration. The listener should never be aware of the machinery.
- Scripture is spoken naturally: "Psalm eighty-two", "the second chapter of
  Acts".
- Assume the hosts are right about their own subject matter. You are reporting
  what they established, not adjudicating it.
- Do not invent detail that is not in the arc. If the arc does not say it, it
  does not go in.

OUTPUT
The narration text only — nothing before it, nothing after it.
