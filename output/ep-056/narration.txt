In this episode, Fathers Andrew and Stephen take us deep into the Orthodox sacrament of chrismation. This is the anointing with holy oil that follows baptism, and it is how we receive the Holy Spirit. They want us to understand that this isn't a separate ritual, but the second half of one single, unified act of initiation. You are baptized into Christ, and then you are anointed to receive the Spirit. It’s one movement into the Christian life.

To really grasp this, they trace the theme of the Spirit’s anointing all the way back through Scripture. It begins in Genesis, where the Spirit of God hovers over the waters of creation like a mother bird brooding over her nest. This is a creative, life-giving presence. Then we see the Spirit filling specific people for specific tasks. Moses had the Spirit to lead Israel. The craftsmen who built the tabernacle were filled with the Spirit to do their artistic work. Kings, like David, were anointed with oil as a sign that God’s Spirit was upon them. And the prophets spoke with that same Spirit.

The prophets also looked forward to a time when this would change. The book of Joel promises that God will pour out His Spirit on all flesh, not just on a chosen few. And that promise is fulfilled at Pentecost in the book of Acts, when the Spirit descends on the apostles and the whole Church is empowered.

Fathers Andrew and Stephen explain that in the time between the Old and New Testaments, people expected several anointed figures, or messiahs. The word messiah simply means “anointed one.” They looked for a kingly messiah, a son of David, to restore the throne. They looked for a priestly messiah, a son of Levi, to restore the priesthood. And they looked for a prophetic messiah, like Elijah, to restore prophecy. The radical claim of the Gospel is that Jesus Christ is all of these. He is the one anointed one who fulfills every role. He is the king, the priest, and the prophet.

This anointing is not just about a title. It’s about power for living. The Holy Spirit empowers Christians to keep the Torah, to follow God’s commandments. This isn’t about earning salvation. It’s about the Spirit producing fruit in us, which is the natural result of the new covenant. The Spirit enables us to do what we could not do on our own.

The hosts also draw a powerful connection between the anointing of the tabernacle and temple and the Christian believer. In the Old Testament, the tabernacle and later the temple were anointed with oil and consecrated so that God’s presence could dwell there. The glory cloud filled the tabernacle, and it filled Solomon’s temple. But the second temple, rebuilt after the exile, was never filled with that same visible glory. The hosts point out that the miraculous signs in the Gospels and Acts show us where the true temple now is. Christ himself is the new temple, and through chrismation, Christians become temples of the Holy Spirit. As Saint Paul writes in First Corinthians, your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit.

This means we are anointed to carry God’s presence into the world. The temple is not just a building, it is a body. And we are that body. We are consecrated, set apart, and empowered to transform our communities simply by carrying the Spirit with us wherever we go. The chrism itself is a specially prepared oil, consecrated by bishops, and it marks us as God’s own. We are anointed ones, messiahs in our own way, sharing in the anointing of Christ, and we are called to live out that reality every day.
