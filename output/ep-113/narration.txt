In this episode, Father Andrew and Father Stephen take us deep into the meaning of the cross. They want to move beyond the common idea that the crucifixion was just about paying a penalty for our sins. Instead, they show it as a great victory over death and the devil. They start with the historical side of things, pointing out that the Persians actually invented crucifixion. The Romans perfected it, using it as a brutal and public deterrent to keep their provinces in line. It was a tool of terror, and Pontius Pilate, the Roman governor of Judea, used it freely to control the population.

To really understand the cross, the hosts turn to the Old Testament. They focus on Deuteronomy chapter twenty-one, where it speaks of the curse of hanging on a tree. This is the foundation for how the New Testament sees Christ’s death. The curse of the Torah, laid out in Leviticus twenty-six and Deuteronomy twenty-eight through thirty, is about the consequences of breaking God’s law. It includes death, corruption, and exile. Father Andrew and Father Stephen stress that Christ’s death was not a penal substitution, where he dies instead of us. Rather, it was a participation in the curse of the Torah. He entered into that curse fully, and by bearing it, he destroyed the power of death and the devil.

They bring in Saint Paul’s words from Galatians chapter three, where he says Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us. This is not about God the Father being angry and needing to be appeased. The hosts explain that the cross is about God condemning sin in the flesh of Christ, as we hear in Romans chapter eight. It is not that Christ himself is condemned, but that sin is dealt with in his body. They also look at Colossians chapter two, where Paul says Christ canceled the record of debt that stood against us, nailing it to the cross. This is not a ransom paid to the devil. It is a public cancellation of the debt of sin and death.

The conversation then turns to the language of redemption and manumission. In the ancient world, manumission was the act of releasing someone from slavery. The hosts use this to explain how Christ’s death sets humanity free from slavery to sin and death. We were in debt slavery, unable to free ourselves, and Christ paid the price to release us. They also connect this to the Old Testament sacrificial system, especially the sin offering. Christ is presented as the ultimate sin offering, consecrated and offered to God for purification. The Day of Atonement, with its two goats, one sacrificed and one sent into the wilderness, foreshadows this work.

Father Andrew and Father Stephen are careful to clarify that Christ’s redemption is universal, but not all are saved. All are raised, but some are raised to condemnation. The victory over death is complete, but our participation in that victory is not automatic. This is where the participatory nature of salvation comes in. The New Testament speaks of being in Christ, of dying and rising with him. It is not just that he died instead of us, but that we die and rise with him. Hebrews chapter two tells us that through his death, Christ destroyed the devil, who holds the power of death, and freed those who were enslaved by the fear of death.

The episode closes with a call to action. We are invited to participate in Christ’s sacrifice through the Eucharist, and by offering our own lives as living sacrifices. We follow Christ through suffering to resurrection. The cross is not just a past event to be remembered. It is a present reality we enter into, a path we walk, and a victory we share.
