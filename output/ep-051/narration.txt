This is a special episode of the Lord of Spirits podcast. Father Stephen De Young is flying solo, because the regular hosts are away. He begins with a quick announcement about an upcoming lecture, and then he gets right into the meat of the show. He’s going to read and analyze an ancient Sumerian epic called “Enmerkar and Ensuhgirana.” It’s a story that dates back to the eighteenth century BC, which puts it right between the time of Abraham and the time of Moses.

The story sets the stage with Enmerkar, the king of Uruk. He’s a legendary figure, the grandfather of Gilgamesh, and he wants to bring the city of Arata under his control. He tries sending letters, but that doesn’t work. So the king of Arata decides to escalate things. He sends his own sorcerer to curse the city of Eresh. The curse is effective, and it hits the city where it hurts. The cows and the goats stop producing milk. This is a big deal, because Eresh is a city associated with Nisaba, the Sumerian goddess of grain, writing, and accounting, who is also tied to cows.

Enmerkar doesn’t take this lying down. He sends his own champion, a wise woman named Sagburu, to confront the sorcerer. The two of them meet on the banks of the Euphrates, which the epic calls the river of the gods. And this is where the wizard duel really gets going. They don’t throw fireballs or lightning bolts. Instead, they engage in magical animal summoning. They create creatures from fish eggs, and each one summons a predator to devour the other’s creation. It’s a bizarre and fascinating contest of power.

Sagburu ultimately gets the upper hand. She prevails, and she kills the sorcerer by removing his vital force, his very life force. With the sorcerer dead, Arata submits to Enmerkar. The city is conquered, not by an army, but by a wise woman’s magic.

Father Stephen doesn’t just leave the story in the ancient past. He draws a direct parallel to the Bible. He points to the contest between Moses and the Egyptian magicians, which you can find in Exodus, chapter seven, verses eight through twelve. In that story, Aaron throws down his staff, and it becomes a serpent. The Egyptian magicians do the same, but Aaron’s serpent swallows theirs. Father Stephen notes that both stories share the same motif. They both feature magical combat that involves summoning animals. It’s a pattern that shows up in ancient literature, and it’s a way of demonstrating power and divine favor.

The episode is a deep dive into a single, strange text. Father Stephen walks through the story carefully, explaining the cultural and political realities behind it. He’s not just telling an old myth. He’s showing how these ancient stories were used to explain the world, and he’s connecting them to the biblical narrative. The duel on the Euphrates is a mirror image of the contest in Pharaoh’s court. In both cases, the power of the true God, or the power of the hero’s patron, wins out over the magic of the enemy.

Father Stephen wraps up by promising that the regular programming will return in October. But for now, he leaves the listener with this strange, ancient tale of a wizard duel, a wise woman’s victory, and a king’s conquest, all played out on the banks of a sacred river.
