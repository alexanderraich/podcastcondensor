In this episode, Fathers Andrew and Stephen begin a new four-part series on the Christology of the Old Testament, and they start with a figure who appears again and again throughout the Hebrew scriptures: the Angel of the Lord. The word "angel" simply means "messenger," and in the Hebrew text, the definite article points to a specific being. The phrase "of the Lord" can be read two ways, either as possessive or as attributive, and this ambiguity opens up a profound mystery. The hosts explore how Second Temple literature, like the Apocalypse of Abraham, develops this figure into an angel who is actually named Yahweh.

The conversation then traces the Angel of the Lord through key moments in the Old Testament. We see him in the burning bush, where he appears to Moses and identifies himself as the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. We see him leading Israel out of Egypt, and we see him in the book of Joshua, where the commander of the Lord’s army appears and receives worship, telling Joshua to remove his sandals because he is standing on holy ground. In Judges, the Angel of the Lord speaks in the first person as Yahweh, rebuking Israel for their disobedience. He appears to Gideon, to Hagar, to Balaam, and to the parents of Samson. He interacts with Elijah, and in Second Kings, he strikes down one hundred and eighty-five thousand Assyrian soldiers in a single night. The hosts point out that this figure is not Saint Michael, because he receives worship and speaks as God himself. Instead, they argue, this is the pre-incarnate Christ, the second person of the Trinity, actively present and working in the history of his people.

This raises a fascinating question. If this is Christ, does he have a body before the incarnation? The hosts affirm that he does, and they explain this by discussing the eternal nature of Christ and the concept of time in heaven. God and the heavenly realm exist outside of time and space, and eternal truths enter into human experience. The incarnation did not change Christ’s divine nature, and his body is the same before and after the resurrection. He is incarnate without change. When God says, "my name is in him," regarding the Angel of the Lord, it means his identity is in the angel, not the literal tetragrammaton.

The hosts also discuss the role of the Angel of the Lord as a warrior and protector. He leads the armies of God, guarding Israel and executing judgment. This is seen in the conquest of Canaan, which they describe as a battle against giants, and in the protection of the people throughout their history. The Torah itself was given through angels, with the Angel of the Presence as the intermediary. This is referenced by Saint Stephen in Acts, by Saint Paul in Galatians, and by the writer of Hebrews. The hosts mention that this was a common belief in the first century, and it is confirmed by the historian Josephus.

The episode concludes with a reflection on how understanding Christ’s active presence in the Old Testament transforms our view of the Gospel. Christ was not just foreshadowed in the Old Testament; he was directly involved in the events themselves. This understanding enriches our reading of scripture and our own lives. The hosts bring in Saint John Chrysostom, who described Christ’s incarnation as a "special ops" mission to defeat the devil, hidden in humility. They also mention Saint Gregory the Theologian, whose real self is his eternal identity in Christ, not his earthly struggles. The Angel of the Lord is a warrior, a protector, and a messenger, and he is the same Lord who walked the earth, died on the cross, and rose again. This is the core teaching of the episode, and it sets the stage for the rest of the series.
